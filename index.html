Abstract

Diffusion- and flow-matching-based text-to-motion models have recently demonstrated impressive capability in generating realistic and diverse 3D human motions from natural language descriptions. However, these approaches rely on multi-step iterative sampling during inference, resulting in high computational cost and significant latency, which limit their applicability in interactive and real-time scenarios. In contrast, one-step text-to-motion generation provides a promising alternative by synthesizing motions in a single forward pass, enabling substantially faster inference. Despite this advantage, existing one-step methods often suffer from a noticeable quality gap compared with iterative generative models, particularly in terms of motion realism and semantic consistency. To address this challenge, we propose MixiMotion, a one-step text-to-motion framework that improves generation quality while maintaining the efficiency of single-step inference. Our method integrates offline teacher-guided supervision with a progressive training curriculum that incrementally enhances motion realism, semantic alignment, and human motion preference, allowing the model to learn high-quality motion priors without costly iterative sampling at inference. On the Structured Semantic Alignment Evaluation (SSAE) benchmark, MixiMotion attains a semantic alignment score of 0.8802, retaining 99.4% of HY-Motion-1.0 full while using 460.0M rather than 1.04B motion-generator parameters and one network evaluation instead of 50.

Method Overview

MixiMotion distills a frozen HY-Motion-1.0-Lite teacher into a direct text-and-noise-to-motion generator. The teacher is evaluated offline to cache diverse targets and text features. The student is optimized by bidirectional set matching and decoded body-space constraints, then refined with balanced behavior lanes and group-aware preferences—all while retaining one network evaluation at inference.

Set matching

Bidirectional teacher-to-student and student-to-teacher coverage preserves multi-sample behavior.

Motion curriculum

Body, foot, contact, stride, root and velocity constraints target natural motion.

Preference optimization

Group-aware candidate scoring selects quality-oriented teacher preferences.

Limitations

MixiMotion is distilled from HY-Motion-1.0-Lite, exact-text rather than semantic near-duplicate decontamination is evaluated, and SSAE depends on a fixed VLM judge over rendered skeleton videos. The human study covers 25 participants and 300 prompts per system.
